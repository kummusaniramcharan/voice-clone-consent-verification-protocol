Honest Limitations & Architecture

Any tool that promises 100% guaranteed deepfake detection is misrepresenting reality. Acoustic forensics is an adversarial cat-and-mouse game. Here is a clear breakdown of where current detectors excel, and where they can fail.

Known Detection Limitations

Crucial technical factors that affect speech classification reliability:

⚡ Rapidly Evolving Generative Models

Text-to-speech models and neural vocoders improve rapidly every few months. Newer diffusion-based speech synthesizers (like Voicebox, StyleTTS2, and modern zero-shot models) generate significantly smoother phase transitions and more natural breathing patterns than older concatenative or autoregressive models, making them progressively harder for static classifiers to distinguish.

📉 Phone Call & WhatsApp Compression

Cellular voice networks (GSM/AMR codecs) and messaging apps (WhatsApp Opus compression) strip audio frequencies above 3,400 Hz or 6,000 Hz to conserve mobile bandwidth. Because neural vocoder artifacts often cluster in higher frequency bands (6 kHz – 16 kHz), aggressive lossy compression blurs these acoustic fingerprints, significantly reducing detection confidence.

🌐 Languages, Accents & Dialects

Most open-source speech deepfake datasets (such as ASVspoof and In-the-Wild) feature predominantly English speakers with North American or European accents. Classification accuracy drops when evaluating tonal languages (Mandarin, Vietnamese), heavily accented regional dialects, or speech containing rapid code-switching.

⚠️ False Positives on Real Human Voices

Natural human speech can occasionally trigger synthetic warnings if the recording contains pitch-correction software (Autotune), heavy dynamic compression, studio noise gates, or if the speaker has a naturally monotone vocal delivery or dysarthria. High microphone clipping can also mimic vocoder square-wave distortion.

📱 Browser Cellular Sandboxing Constraints

Under Android 10+ and iOS privacy architecture, third-party web apps cannot secretly record active cellular phone calls. The Live Scam Tracker relies on honest input channels: microphone speakerphone capture, tab/system audio sharing (getDisplayMedia) for web calls, or our standalone Android CallScreening companion service.

Why We Cap Displayed Certainty at 95%

In VoiceClone, the circular probability gauge is strictly capped at a maximum of 95%, and scores between 35% and 65% are marked as Inconclusive.

Displaying "100% AI" or "100% Real" creates dangerous false confidence. If a victim relies entirely on an automated detector to dismiss an actual family emergency, or conversely trusts an AI caller that slipped through, catastrophic harm can result. Automated detection must always be paired with operational verification (such as calling back on a verified number or asking a family safe-word).

Underlying Architecture

VoiceClone uses a transparent, client-side digital signal processing (DSP) and trained classification pipeline running inside browser Web Workers:

  • DSP Acoustic Feature Extraction: 24 physical cues computed per clip: recording noise floor and native bandwidth, Hann-windowed FFT spectral flatness, centroid, 95% rolloff, 4-8 kHz energy, spectral flux CV, 26-filter MFCC temporal variance and delta, normalized autocorrelation pitch (70-400 Hz), F0 mean, semitone range, flat pitch steps (<0.5 Hz plateaus), harmonics-to-noise ratio (HNR), vocal shimmer, and pause cadence dynamics.
  • On-Device Training & Validation: L2-regularized logistic regression trained via gradient descent (500 epochs, λ=0.01, η=0.1, balanced class weights). Evaluated with Grouped Cross-Validation by audio source so speakers/microphones never leak between training and test sets.
  • Zero Upload Guarantee: All audio decoding, feature extraction, and training run strictly inside browser memory and IndexedDB. No audio is ever uploaded anywhere.
